Course Overview

About This Course

This course builds on the concepts learned in principles of macroeconomics to develop a deeper comprehension of the theoretical foundations of the field, and to apply those models to real-world analysis. Extensive references to current events come up throughout, and students apply the concepts learned during the semester to surrounding policy issues.

Learning Objectives

Students will develop competence with these tools to understand the determination of aggregate output, unemployment, prices, and growth, and to analyze the impacts of fiscal and monetary policy on the macroeconomy. By the end of the course, students should be competent in the following areas:

  • The measurement of GDP as expenditure, income, and output
  • The measurement and cost of unemployment
  • The measurement and cost of inflation
  • The analysis of the macroeconomy in the short run using the IS-LM model
  • The analysis of the macroeconomy in the medium run using the IS-LM-PC model
  • The analysis of the macroeconomy in the long run using the Solow growth model, and the measurement of technical progress using growth accounting
  • The effects of monetary and fiscal policy
  • The analysis of open economy macroeconomics and exchange rate determination

Prerequisites

Principles of Macroeconomics (ECON 204) and Calculus (MATH 141, MATH 155, or MATH 160).

Course Policies

Attendance & Participation

You are expected to attend every class and participate in in-class discussions. Activities consist of in-class analysis of articles, current events, and similar work, completed in groups or solo. No activity appears on the course schedule, so regular attendance is the only way to complete them.

Late Work

Any and all late submissions receive a 10% penalty for each day they are late, unless other arrangements have been made between the instructor and the student.

Make-Up Exams

Make-up exams are not permitted except under extraordinary circumstances. Exam dates are obligations and should be treated as such.

Artificial Intelligence

Just as with the calculator and the personal computer, new technologies should be responsibly integrated to increase productivity and complement the learning process, not replace it. Use of AI is expected and encouraged as a personal tutor — the technology has its limits, so be cautious. To ensure academic integrity, more emphasis is placed on closed-book examinations, in-class participation, and an oral component.

Communication

Please communicate through Canvas or email, and include the course number in the subject line. I will do my best to respond within 36 to 48 hours. If you have missed class, reach out as soon as you are able so we can get you up to speed on course material and any assigned materials.

Illness & Accommodations

Please note that the CSU Health Network does not provide excuse notes for students who miss classes, assignments, or tests because of short-term illness, injury, or mental health problems. Students who require accommodations should contact Student Disability Services as early in the semester as possible. I will do my best to be accommodating in an equitable manner as life throws unexpected challenges toward all of us.

Academic Integrity

Collaboration on problem sets is encouraged, but each student must submit their own work. All exam work must be completed independently.

Classroom Norms

We listen to each other with the intent to understand different perspectives, ask questions when we have them, and make sure everyone's voice is heard. When debating policies and theories, we address the merits of the arguments and not the personal qualities of the individual supporting or opposing the topic at hand.
