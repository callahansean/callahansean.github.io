Course Overview

About This Course

Description

Environmental Economics applies the tools of microeconomics to understand environmental problems and evaluate potential solutions. The course focuses on the economic causes of environmental degradation, the design and evaluation of environmental policy, and the trade-offs between economic activity and environmental quality.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • Identify and analyze market failures leading to environmental problems
  • Evaluate the efficiency and equity of different environmental policy instruments
  • Apply cost-benefit analysis to environmental policy decisions
  • Understand the economics of natural resources and sustainability
  • Critically assess current environmental policy debates

Prerequisites

ECON 202 (Principles of Microeconomics) or equivalent. Some familiarity with basic algebra is expected.

Curriculum

Course Topics

01
Market Failures & Externalities

Why markets fail to account for environmental costs. Negative externalities, public goods, and the commons problem.

02
Policy Instruments

Comparing regulatory approaches: Pigouvian taxes, cap-and-trade, standards, and subsidies. Efficiency, equity, and political economy.

03
Cost-Benefit Analysis

Valuing environmental goods and services. Methods for placing monetary values on non-market outcomes.

04
Natural Resources

Economics of renewable and non-renewable resources. Optimal extraction paths, sustainability, and intergenerational equity.

05
Climate Economics

The economics of climate change. Social cost of carbon, international agreements, and the macroeconomics of the green transition.

06
Environmental Justice

Distributional consequences of environmental problems and policies. Race, income, and the unequal burden of pollution.

Course Policies

Attendance

Regular attendance is expected. Much of the course involves in-class discussion and applied problem-solving that cannot be replicated from notes alone.

Late Work

Late work is not accepted unless prior arrangements have been made with the instructor before the due date.

Academic Integrity

Collaboration on problem sets is encouraged, but each student must submit their own written work. All exam and written assignment submissions must be your own.

Accommodations

Students who require accommodations should contact Student Disability Services and share documentation with me as early as possible in the semester.
